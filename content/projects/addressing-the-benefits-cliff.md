Means-tested benefits are supposed to phase out as income rises — the problem is how abruptly. A modest raise can trigger a disproportionate loss of SNAP, Medicaid, or housing assistance all at once, so that taking the raise leaves a family worse off in total resources than turning it down would have. That's the "benefits cliff," and it's not a side effect of anti-poverty programs; it's a structural flaw in how eligibility thresholds are built.

The piece walks through the mechanics using a single parent on minimum wage juggling SNAP, Medicaid, and childcare assistance — the exact person these programs are designed to help, caught by the exact design meant to help them. The quiet argument underneath it: a safety net that punishes the first real step off of it isn't stabilizing anyone, it's just changing which kind of stuck they're in.
