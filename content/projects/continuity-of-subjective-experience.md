Traditional care ethics grounds moral obligation in altruism — we care for the vulnerable because our lives are relationally enmeshed with theirs. This paper argues that framing is vulnerable to exactly the critique care ethics usually draws: unbounded obligation, expected self-sacrifice. The alternative argued here starts somewhere colder, and I think more defensible: perceiving another person is itself a form of objectification, and objectifying someone introduces a destabilization risk — to them, and through them, back to the continuity of your own subjective experience. Care isn't owed because the Other is vulnerable. It's owed because their destabilization is a live threat to yours.

It's a short, dense argument, and the direct predecessor to the "promiration" framework developed afterward.
