Ohio has thousands of vacant and poorly managed rental properties sitting alongside a real affordable-housing shortage, with very little connecting the two. OHARP proposes closing that gap with three linked mechanisms: a local-agent mandate modeled on Cleveland's Residents First program (any out-of-state or non-adjacent-county landlord must appoint someone in Ohio who's accountable for code violations), a graduated vacancy tax on properties left empty past six months, and a statewide rental registry to actually enforce both.

The revenue side is what makes it more than a punitive measure. Vacancy-tax proceeds fund tax credits for developers converting vacant properties into affordable units, rental assistance for low-income families, and grants for community land trusts and historic preservation. The goal isn't to punish landlords for its own sake — it's to make holding a property vacant more expensive than putting it to use.
