Universities, hospitals, and social-service agencies that receive federal funding collect enormous amounts of personal data — and in the absence of a comprehensive federal privacy law, plenty of it gets shared or sold to third parties with little oversight and less consent. This brief proposes a direct lever: any organization receiving government grants, contracts, or subsidies would be barred from sharing personally identifiable information for non-essential purposes, with narrow exemptions for law enforcement, public-health reporting, and IRB-governed research.

Enforcement runs through the funding agencies themselves — Education, HHS, and others — in coordination with the Department of Justice, with funding suspension as the actual teeth behind the requirement. It's a narrower ask than a GDPR-style federal privacy law, and that's the point: it uses leverage the federal government already has, rather than waiting on a broader law that hasn't materialized.
