Ohio's most underserved counties — Scioto, Vinton, Meigs, and others in Appalachian Ohio — carry some of the highest rates of poverty, substance use, and unmet mental-health need in the state, and chronic trouble keeping licensed professionals in the field long enough to make a difference. CASSI proposes up to $50,000 in loan forgiveness over ten years, at 10% per year of full-time qualifying work, for social workers, mental-health counselors, substance-abuse counselors, nonprofit-management professionals, and prevention specialists who work in those counties.

The funding mechanism is the more interesting design choice: rather than competing for general-fund dollars, CASSI is built to draw $30–45 million annually from a tax on recreational marijuana sales — treating workforce retention in social services as a direct claim on that revenue rather than an afterthought.
