Coal built entire economies in Appalachia, and the market's move away from it hasn't come with a plan for what replaces the jobs, the tax base, or the sense of place that came with it. This piece treats that as the actual policy question — not whether decarbonization happens, but whether it happens in a way that rebuilds the region or just leaves another extractive industry's exit behind it.

The case for clean energy, sustainable manufacturing, and rural economic revitalization here isn't environmental first — it's economic first, with the climate benefit as the part that also happens to be true. It's the same instinct behind CarbonSmart's small-business and student grant programs, written before that policy proposal existed: decarbonization only works politically in a region like this if it's designed to put something back.
