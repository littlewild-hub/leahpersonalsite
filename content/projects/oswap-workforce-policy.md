Ohio's labor shortages in construction, healthcare, renewable energy, and advanced manufacturing aren't going to close themselves, and a four-year degree isn't the right answer for a lot of people who could fill them. OSWAP proposes tax credits for businesses that hire certified vocational workers, additional credits layered on top for apprenticeship creation, mentorship, and retention, and a further bonus for doing any of it in a rural or underserved county.

The design leans on stacking: a business hiring an apprentice in an underserved region can combine the base credit, the rural bonus, the mentorship credit, and a retention bonus at the one-year mark — meant to make the program worth the paperwork rather than a token gesture. Oversight sits with a task force under Ohio's Department of Labor, funded initially through workforce-development reallocation and federal apprenticeship grants.
