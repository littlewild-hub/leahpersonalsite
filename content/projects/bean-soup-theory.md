A viral TikTok video about bean soup, and one commenter's "what if I don't like beans?", became a genuine case study for a cultural rhetoric class: what looks like a joke about self-centered comment sections turns out to be a clean three-stage pipeline, from a surface-level phenomenon (Bean Soup Theory) to a tactic (whataboutism, shifting a conversation laterally instead of engaging it) to an ideology (anti-intellectualism, dismissing anything that doesn't personally apply).

The paper takes the joke seriously enough to trace where it actually leads: algorithm-curated feeds train users to expect content built specifically for them, and anything that isn't gets read as irrelevant or wrong rather than simply not-for-me — the same mechanism that lets expertise get waved off as "not that deep."
