The origin is genuinely small: a woman posted a recipe for an iron-rich bean soup she eats on her period, aimed at anemic girls, and the comments filled with some version of "what if I don't like beans?" — missing the demographic the video was for entirely. The internet's pile-on ("it's not all about you") became its own viral moment, and in the process gave a name to a specific, previously unnamed rhetorical move: content made for a particular audience gets received by someone outside that audience as though it were a universal claim, and rejected on those terms.

Cultural rhetoric analysis — which studies how meaning gets constructed through everyday communication practices rather than formal texts — is the method the paper uses to trace where that move actually leads, and it's a clean three-stage pipeline. Bean Soup Theory is the surface phenomenon: algorithm-curated feeds train users to expect content built specifically for them, so anything that isn't reads as irrelevant rather than simply not-for-me. That escalates into whataboutism as a tactic — shifting a conversation laterally to a different population or comparison rather than engaging the actual claim, the same rhetorical move used to derail conversations about, say, one administration's immigration record by pointing at another's. And that, sustained, becomes anti-intellectualism as the underlying ideology: "if it doesn't apply to me, it should; and if it shouldn't, then it doesn't matter" — the same dismissal that lets creators with real subject-matter expertise get waved off as "not that deep" simply because their content wasn't tailored to the viewer.

The paper doesn't let the analysis go uncomplicated. Because Bean Soup Theory is a non-academic, loosely-defined internet concept, it's also vulnerable to being weaponized the same way terms like "gaslighting" or "fascism" have been — including against people asking, in good faith, to be included in a conversation or to have it made accessible. Naming a phenomenon doesn't settle who gets to invoke it, and the paper treats that tension as part of the finding rather than a loose end to ignore.
