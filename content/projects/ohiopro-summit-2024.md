A panel discussion on what it actually takes to caregive for a young person moving through more than one system at once — child welfare, juvenile justice, behavioral health — each with its own paperwork, its own definition of progress, and no shared account of what the caregiver is absorbing in between them.

This was early field-testing of the argument that later became TRACEs: that multi-systemic caregiving is itself a trauma exposure, not just a stressful logistics problem, and that treating it as the latter is part of why caregivers burn out faster than the systems around them adjust.
