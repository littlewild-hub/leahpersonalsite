Most advisory councils are built to be listened to politely and then overruled. The Survivor Voices Council was built to inform organizational policy, training, and program strategy directly, which meant the design work happened before the first meeting: trauma-informed onboarding, facilitation structures, and a participatory governance model built specifically to distribute power equitably rather than default back to whoever already had it in the room.

The harder part wasn't the structure — it was staying honest about perception asymmetry: what looks like safe engagement from the organization's side of the table can read very differently from a survivor's. Getting that right meant building CE Broker-approved curricula and technical-assistance resources alongside the council itself, so the multidisciplinary professionals working with it had a shared, trauma-informed baseline going in.
