Rotary dues run $150 to $300 a year plus per-meeting costs; the Chamber's basic tier is $300 to $600. Median weekly pay for a 20-to-24-year-old is around $672, against $1.6 trillion in collective student debt. Read against those numbers, declining Gen Z membership in civic clubs stops looking like apathy and starts looking like an accurate read of who these organizations were actually built for: business owners and established professionals with money and a free weekday lunch hour, not a generation that entered the workforce through a recession or a pandemic.

The piece's real argument is that the civic engagement is still there, just unmeasured by institutions built for an older economy — volunteering, mutual aid, digital organizing, the forms of showing up that don't require a membership fee to count. The fix isn't a lecture about civic duty; it's sliding-scale dues, meetings that aren't all weekday lunches, and subsidized memberships for people who'd show up if showing up were actually affordable.
