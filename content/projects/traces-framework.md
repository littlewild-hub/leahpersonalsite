TRACEs — Trauma & Relational Adversity in Caregiving Environments — started from a gap I kept running into as a Family Peer Supporter: the tools available for assessing a family measured the child's symptoms and the system's compliance, but nothing measured what the caregiving itself was doing to the caregiver. Multi-system-involved families carry relational strain, burnout, and trauma load that standard intake and treatment-planning instruments don't have a column for.

TRACEs is a six-domain assessment built to name that directly, drawing on care ethics, cognitive load theory, and vulnerability analysis rather than treating caregiver burnout as an incidental footnote to the child's case. The domains overlap by design — caregiving trauma doesn't sit in one box, and a framework that pretends it does will miss the interaction effects that actually explain why a family in seemingly manageable circumstances is close to collapse.

It has been self-directed work since 2023, tested and refined against the field rather than written in the abstract: presented as a poster at UConn's Training Institutes, and as full sessions to provider learning communities and caregiver summits across 2025 and 2026. Each presentation has sharpened a domain or exposed where the language wasn't landing with the people who would actually use it.
